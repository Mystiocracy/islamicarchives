Taqī al-Dīn al-Nabhānī was born in 1909 in Ijzim, near Haifa in Ottoman and then Mandatory Palestine, to a family of scholars. His maternal grandfather was the poet and jurist Yūsuf al-Nabhānī. He studied at al-Azhar and at Dār al-ʿUlūm in Cairo, then taught and served as a judge in Palestine. After 1948 he settled in Jordan and Lebanon and worked in the Islamic courts.

In 1953 he founded Hizb ut-Tahrir in Jerusalem, a party that aims to restore the caliphate and to rule by Islamic law. He wrote its key texts, among them *Niẓām al-Islām* (The System of Islam), *al-Dawla al-Islāmiyya* (The Islamic State), *al-Shakhṣiyya al-Islāmiyya* (The Islamic Personality) and *Muqaddimat al-dustūr* (Introduction to the Constitution). These set out a comprehensive theory of Islamic government, economics and society. He spent his later years in Beirut, where he died in 1977.

Hizb ut-Tahrir is banned in several countries. The archive lists his writings as works of political thought, and their inclusion is no endorsement of the movement. Most of his works are published free of charge by the party's own sites.
