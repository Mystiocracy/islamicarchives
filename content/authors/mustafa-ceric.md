Mustafa Cerić was born in 1952 in the village of Veliko Čajno near Visoko, in what was then Yugoslavia. He studied at the Gazi Husrev-beg Madrasa in Sarajevo and then at al-Azhar University in Cairo, and worked as an imam before moving in 1981 to the Islamic Cultural Center of Greater Chicago. During his years in the United States he learned English and took a doctorate in Islamic studies at the University of Chicago, after which he returned to Yugoslavia and served as an imam in Zagreb.

In 1993, during the war in Bosnia, he became head of the Islamic Community of Bosnia and Herzegovina, holding the office of Grand Mufti (reis-ul-ulema) until 2012, when Husein Kavazović succeeded him. He was a signatory of the 2007 open letter A Common Word Between Us and You, received several international awards for interfaith work, and in 2012 helped found the World Bosniak Congress, of which he became president. He has argued that Islam is a strong element of Bosniak national identity, and in 2014 he stood unsuccessfully for the Bosniak seat on the Bosnian Presidency.

His books, in Bosnian and English, include a scholarly study of the theology of the Hanafi-Maturidi tradition, writings on Islamic ethics and on the place of Muslims in Bosnia and in Europe, and collections of speeches.
