Said Nursi was born in 1877 in the village of Nurs, in the Bitlis province of eastern Anatolia, to a Kurdish family. He studied in a series of local madrasas without completing a formal course, but his memory and skill in debate earned him the title *Bediüzzaman*, 'wonder of the age'. In Van, under the patronage of the provincial governor, he also read modern science, and he became convinced that religious and modern learning should be taught together; he spent years pursuing a plan for a university in the east, the Medresetü'z-Zehra.

In his early career, which he later called the period of the 'Old Said', he was politically active in Istanbul, supported constitutional government, and was tried and acquitted after the 1909 counter-revolution. In the First World War he commanded volunteer militia on the Caucasus front, was captured by Russian forces, and escaped back to Istanbul in 1918. Works of this period include *Rational Arguments*, *The Debates*, *The Damascus Sermon* and the Qur'anic commentary *Signs of Miraculousness*.

After the founding of the Turkish Republic he withdrew from politics. Exiled in 1926 to Barla in south-western Anatolia, he began the *Risale-i Nur* ('Treatises of Light'), a vast commentary on the Qur'an's teachings on God, prophethood and the afterlife, written in Turkish for ordinary believers and copied by hand by his students. Over the following decades he was repeatedly tried, imprisoned and kept under surveillance, but his works circulated widely. The *Risale-i Nur* tried to answer secular materialism through reflection on the created world rather than through politics, and it became the basis of the Nur movement, one of the largest faith-based movements in modern Turkey.

He died in Urfa on 23 March 1960. Months later, after the military coup, his body was removed and reburied at an undisclosed location.
