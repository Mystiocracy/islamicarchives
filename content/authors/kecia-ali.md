Kecia Ali is an American scholar of Islam. She studied history and feminist studies at Stanford and completed her master's degree and doctorate in religion at Duke University. She embraced Islam while a student.

She has held research and fellowship positions at Brandeis University and Harvard Divinity School and teaches religion at Boston University, where her work centres on classical Islamic jurisprudence, ethics, gender and the writing of religious biography. She has served as president of the Society for the Study of Muslim Ethics.

Her books examine how early legal texts treated marriage, sex and slavery, how Muslim authors have narrated the lives of Muhammad and of the jurist al-Shafi'i, and how contemporary feminist ethics can engage the sources. She has also written on the ethics of popular fiction.
