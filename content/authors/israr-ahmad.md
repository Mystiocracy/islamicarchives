Israr Ahmad was born on 26 April 1932 in Hisar, in the Punjab province of British India (now in Haryana, India). His family moved to Montgomery (now Sahiwal) and then to Pakistan after Partition. In 1950 he went to Lahore to study medicine at King Edward Medical College and qualified as a doctor (MBBS) in 1954. In 1965 he also completed a master's degree in Islamic Studies at the University of Karachi. He practised medicine only briefly. From early on, teaching the Qur'an became his main work.

As a student he was drawn to Abul A'la Mawdudi's thought. He became a leading organiser of Islami Jamiat-e-Talaba and then a member of Jamaat-e-Islami. In 1957 he broke with the Jamaat after it decided to contest elections. In his view, entering electoral politics compromised the movement's original programme of intellectual and moral reform. He went on to set out his own idea of revival, which was a return to the Qur'an as the basis for renewing faith among Muslims. In 1972 he founded the Markazi Anjuman Khuddam-ul-Quran in Lahore to promote the study and teaching of the Qur'an. Its centre, the Quran Academy in Model Town, Lahore, became the base for his courses and publications. In 1975 he founded Tanzeem-e-Islami, an organisation built on personal pledges of allegiance (bay'ah), whose stated goal was to establish Islam as a complete social and political order. He led it until 2002, when poor health made him step down.

Television made him famous across Pakistan. His first programme, *Al-Kitab*, aired during Ramadan in 1978. It was followed on PTV by *Alif Lam Mim*, *Rasul-e-Kamil*, *Umm-ul-Kitab* and *Al-Huda*, and the weekly broadcasts of the early 1980s reached a very large audience. He received the Sitara-e-Imtiaz in 1981. His complete lecture commentary on the whole Qur'an, recorded in 1998 and published as *Bayan-ul-Quran*, is still his best-known body of work. He also developed the *Muntakhab Nisab*, a selected syllabus of Qur'anic passages used in study circles. He wrote or dictated around sixty books and booklets in Urdu, and many of them were translated into English and other languages.

His political thought centred on reviving the *khilafah*. He argued that a just Islamic order should be established through a disciplined, non-violent movement modelled on the Prophet's own method of revolution. In 1991 he launched the Tehreek-e-Khilafat to campaign for this goal in Pakistan. He died of heart failure at his home in Lahore on 14 April 2010.

His books are published by the Markazi Anjuman Khuddam-ul-Quran and Tanzeem-e-Islami, which make them free to download; the archive links to those copies and to his recorded lectures.
