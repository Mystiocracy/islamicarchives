Tariq Ramadan was born in Geneva in 1962. His father, Saʿid Ramadan, was a leader of the Muslim Brotherhood in exile, and his maternal grandfather was Hasan al-Banna, the movement's founder. He studied at the University of Geneva, taking a master’s in French literature and a doctorate in Arabic and Islamic studies, and he also studied classical Islamic sciences in Cairo.

He became widely known for arguing that Muslims in Europe and North America should see themselves as full citizens, not as a minority apart. In books such as *To Be a European Muslim* (1999) and *Western Muslims and the Future of Islam* (2004) he argued for a "Western Muslim" identity rooted in the sources of Islam and in the societies where Muslims live. His *Radical Reform: Islamic Ethics and Liberation* (2009) called for reform that begins with the interpretation of ethics. He was a visiting fellow at St Antony’s College, Oxford, from 2005 and Professor of Contemporary Islamic Studies there from 2009, and he lectured widely in Europe and the Muslim world. His public positions were often debated, and in 2004 the United States revoked his visa before he could take up a post at Notre Dame.

From October 2017 he was accused of rape and sexual assault by several women, and he took leave from Oxford in November 2017. In Switzerland, a Geneva appeals court convicted him of rape and sexual coercion in September 2024, and the conviction became final in 2025. In France he was tried in his absence and convicted in March 2026, a judgment that may be appealed. He has denied the allegations, though in 2018 he acknowledged consensual relations with some of the women. This archive lists his writings as intellectual works and does not assess those proceedings.

His books remain in copyright. His own website, tariqramadan.com, no longer works, so the archive links only to the few texts that are freely available elsewhere.
