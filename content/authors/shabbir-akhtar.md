Shabbir Akhtar was a British Muslim philosopher of religion, born in Pakistan in 1960. He read philosophy at the University of Cambridge and completed a doctorate in the philosophy of religion at the University of Calgary in 1984. He taught philosophy at the International Islamic University Malaysia and later at Old Dominion University in the United States, and from 2012 was associated with the Faculty of Theology and Religions at the University of Oxford. He died in Oxford in July 2023.

His work dealt with faith and secular modernity, comparing how Islam and Christianity have responded to it, as well as pluralism and race relations in Britain. He came to wide public attention with *Be Careful with Muhammad!* (1989), a response to the Salman Rushdie affair. Other books include *The Light in the Enlightenment* (1990), *The Final Imperative: An Islamic Theology of Liberation* (1991), *The Quran and the Secular Mind* (2008), *Islam as Political Religion* (2010) and *The New Testament in Muslim Eyes: Paul's Letter to the Galatians* (2018). He also published several collections of poetry.

He contributed a number of papers to Yaqeen Institute.
