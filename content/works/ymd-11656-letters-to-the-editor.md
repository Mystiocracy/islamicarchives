**Q: Interest, or usury, is prohibited in Islam. As we are citizens of a non-Islamic country, whose economy is based on interest and capitalism, directly, or indirectly, we Muslims have to be a part and parcel of interest-based transaction.**

**Secondly, what about being employed in a Government bank? How is such an employee different from other government employees? Corporates, business houses avail loans on interest from banks to run and grow their businesses. How about being employed there?**

***Sabir Yaragudri,***  
*On Email*

**YMD**

The economy -- in general -- is turning 'interest-based.' It keeps marching forward so that it is now influencing non-financial sectors also. The world of those who treat interest as unlawful is shrinking fast. It has been so predicted by no less than the Prophet himself. However, the same Prophet has predicted that a group of his *Ummah* will always remain true to their faith. None will do these Muslims any harm.

We invite the faith-conscious members of the *Ummah* to remain steadfast, avoiding *riba*-dealings, and *riba*-transactions -- to the extent of their knowledge.

When, upon study (which is incumbent upon an active Muslim), a transaction is revealed to be *riba*-based, they should avoid it. What reaches of it their nozzles, is unavoidable, and is hoped to be pardonable.

A bank is an organization whose main business is money, therefore, employment with them is prohibited. If someone is already in its employment, he may be on the lookout for an alternative. Allah's help is obtainable.

As regards "Corporates, business houses who avail loans on interest from banks to run and grow their businesses," if their business is predominantly in financial dealings, almost like banks, then, it is preferable not to work with them.
