Ahmad von Denffer

b. 1949 · Germany · Researcher and author on the sciences of the Qurʾān

Ahmad von Denffer was born in Germany in 1949. He studied Islamic studies and social anthropology at the University of Mainz, with additional courses in missiology, and has a particular interest in Christian–Muslim relations. In 1978 he joined The Islamic Foundation in Leicester as a research fellow, and he later worked with the Islamic Centre in Munich.

His best-known book, ʿUlūm al-Qurʾān: An Introduction to the Sciences of the Qurʾān (The Islamic Foundation, 1983), was written as an English-language introduction to the subject. It covers revelation, the transmission and collection of the Qurʾānic text, manuscripts and printing, exegesis, Orientalist scholarship, translations, and recitation. The Quran Hub’s account of how the Qurʾān was compiled follows its chapters on transmission, manuscripts and print.

The book’s own author note lists other titles, including a bibliography of literature on hadith in European languages, a German translation of al-Nawawī’s Forty Ḥadīth, and children’s and introductory books. Only the work listed below has been checked against its edition so far.
