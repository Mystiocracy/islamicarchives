Fatima (Fatema) Mernissi was born in 1940 in Fez, Morocco, into a well-to-do family, and grew up in a household where women lived in a domestic harem, an experience she later made the subject of her memoir. She belonged to one of the first generations of Moroccan girls to attend nationalist-founded schools, studied political science at Mohammed V University in Rabat, and continued in sociology at the Sorbonne and at Brandeis University in the United States, where she received her doctorate in 1973.

Returning to Morocco, she taught at Mohammed V University and later held a research post at its University Institute for Scientific Research, while also carrying out studies for international agencies. Her dissertation, published as *Beyond the Veil* (1975), analysed gender relations in Moroccan society and argued that Muslim traditions regarded female sexuality as an active force requiring social control. In *Le Harem politique* (1987; English *The Veil and the Male Elite*) she turned to early Islamic sources, questioning the transmission and use of certain hadith about women and presenting the Prophet's community as more open to women's participation than later jurists allowed. *Sultanes oubliées* (1990; *The Forgotten Queens of Islam*) recovered women who had exercised political power in Muslim history.

In the 1990s she addressed the relationship between Islam, democracy and the West in *La Peur-modernité* (*Islam and Democracy: Fear of the Modern World*), wrote the widely translated memoir *Dreams of Trespass* (1994), and explored Western fantasies of the harem in *Scheherazade Goes West* (2001). She also documented civil-society initiatives in rural Morocco and encouraged younger writers through collaborative workshops.

Mernissi received the Prince of Asturias Award for Letters in 2003 and the Erasmus Prize in 2004. She died in Rabat in 2015. Her historical reading of the sources made her a founding figure of Muslim feminist scholarship, though her methods and conclusions have also been widely debated.
