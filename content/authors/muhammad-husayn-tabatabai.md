Muhammad Husayn Tabataba'i was born in Tabriz around 1903. He studied the Qur'an and Persian literature as a child and then Arabic, jurisprudence and legal theory at a Tabriz school. He went on to Najaf, where he spent about ten years studying with leading jurists, philosophers and mystics, among them Muhammad-Husayn Na'ini, Muhammad-Husayn Gharawi Isfahani and Sayyid Ali Qadi.

After returning to Iran he farmed for some years before moving to Qom in 1946. There he taught philosophy, Qur'anic exegesis and the Islamic sciences, and held a long series of conversations with the French scholar Henry Corbin, which were later published. His students included Morteza Motahhari, Seyyed Hossein Nasr and Hussein-Ali Montazeri, and he exerted a lasting influence on Shia philosophy and exegesis in the twentieth century. He died in 1981.

His great work is Tafsir al-Mizan, a commentary on the Qur'an written in Arabic over about two decades. He also wrote The Principles of Philosophy and the Method of Realism, textbooks of Islamic metaphysics such as Bidayat al-Hikma, essays and a book on Shia history and belief that appeared in English as Shi'ite Islam. He also wrote poetry in Persian and Arabic.
