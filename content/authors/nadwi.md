Sayyid Abul Ḥasan ʿAlī Ḥasanī Nadwī, widely known as Ali Miyan, was born in Rae Bareli and educated at the Nadwatul Ulama in Lucknow, which he later led as rector. Writing in Arabic and Urdu, he became one of the most widely read Muslim scholars of the twentieth century; his *Mādhā khasira al-ʿālam bi-inḥiṭāṭ al-Muslimīn* (What the World Lost by the Decline of Muslims) made his name across the Arab world.

His works range over the history of reform and revival, the life of the Prophet, education, spirituality and the Muslim encounter with the modern West, alongside many speeches and booklets. He received the King Faisal International Prize in 1980. He died in Rae Bareli in 1999.

His works remain in copyright; the archive lists every edition that can be downloaded free, mostly from the Sayyid Abul Hasan Ali Nadwi Centre, in Urdu, Arabic, English and many other languages.
