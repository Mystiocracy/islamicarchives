Yasir Qadhi was born on 30 January 1975 in Houston, Texas, to Pakistani parents. His father, a physician, had come from Karachi in 1963 and was among the first foreign-born Muslims to settle in Houston. When Yasir was about five the family moved to Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, where he grew up and, by his own account, memorised the Qur'an as a teenager. He took a BSc in chemical engineering at the University of Houston, then went to the Islamic University of Madinah (his own website says in 1995; Wikipedia says 1996), where he earned a diploma in Arabic, a BA in hadith studies and an MA in Islamic theology.

He took a PhD in religious studies at Yale in 2013 with a dissertation on Ibn Taymiyya's *Darʾ taʿāruḍ al-ʿaql wa-l-naql* (*Reconciling Reason and Revelation in the Writings of Ibn Taymiyya*); it is catalogued under the name Yasir Kazi. He taught religious studies at Rhodes College in Memphis, served as dean of academic affairs at the AlMaghrib Institute, and was long associated with the Memphis Islamic Center. He is now dean of The Islamic Seminary of America and resident scholar of the East Plano Islamic Center in Texas, and chairs the Fiqh Council of North America.

Qadhi began his career in the Salafi tradition of his Madinah training. After 2001 and during his doctoral years he began to question parts of it, and from the mid-2010s he distanced himself from the label, describing his position as a middle-way (*wasaṭī*) Sunni one. He has said so in lectures and in his book *Understanding Salafism* (Oneworld, 2025). Scholars have studied him as an example of this shift. In 2016 the Islamic State's magazine *Dabiq* denounced him after he criticised the group. He has also been criticised at various times: for remarks about Jews and the Holocaust in 2008 and 2010, which he later retracted, and for a 2020 remark about "holes in the narrative" of the Qur'an's preservation, which he later clarified.

His books are mostly popular works on the Qur'an, the Prophet's biography, supplication and the heart, published by Kube and others; his scholarly writing is on Ibn Taymiyya and on Salafi–Ashʿarī disputes. His lecture series on YouTube (Seerah, Stories of the Prophets, Heart Matters and others) are among his most widely watched work. The archive links to the free copies and to his recorded lectures.
