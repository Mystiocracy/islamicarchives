Abdal Hakim Murad (T. J. Winter)

عبد الحكيم مراد

b. 1960 · United Kingdom · Theologian, translator, essayist, educator

Timothy John Winter, known in Muslim contexts as Abdal Hakim Murad, was born in London in 1960 to an architect father and a painter mother. He was educated at Westminster School and embraced Islam in 1979, while still a young man. He read Arabic at Pembroke College, Cambridge, graduating with a double first in 1983, and then spent several years studying the traditional Islamic sciences at al-Azhar in Cairo and with scholars in Saudi Arabia and Yemen, before learning Turkish and Persian in London. He later completed a doctorate at the Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam.

Winter’s scholarly work began with translations of classical texts, notably two books of al-Ghazālī’s Iḥyāʾ ʿulūm al-dīn and al-Bayhaqī’s Seventy-Seven Branches of Faith. He became Shaykh Zayed Lecturer in Islamic Studies in the Faculty of Divinity at Cambridge, edited The Cambridge Companion to Classical Islamic Theology (2008), and in 2009 founded the Cambridge Muslim College, which trains imams and Muslim scholars for service in Britain. He also led the project to build the Cambridge Central Mosque, which opened in 2019.

As Abdal Hakim Murad he is widely read as an essayist. Writing from within Sunni tradition, with its schools of law, Ashʿarī and Māturīdī theology and Sufi spirituality, he has defended the classical madhhabs against modern literalism, criticised both secular liberalism and Islamist radicalism as products of modernity, and argued that terrorism carried out in Islam’s name is a break with that tradition. Essays such as ‘Understanding the Four Madhhabs’, ‘Bombing Without Moonlight’ and ‘British and Muslim?’, many of them first published online, circulated widely among English-speaking Muslims.

A recurring theme of his work is the possibility of a European and British Islam rooted in local culture and in continuity with the wider Muslim heritage, explored in Travelling Home: Essays on Islam in Europe (2020). His aphoristic Contentions and their commentary display his literary style and his interest in Christian-Muslim relations.
