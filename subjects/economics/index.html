Economics

Islamic economics, finance and social justice.

Islamic economics took shape as a discipline in the 1940s, partly as an answer to socialism and capitalism. Maududi and Sayyid Qutb set out the principles, and Muhammad Baqir al-Sadr's Our Economics (1961) tried to work out a whole system. From the 1970s attention moved to practice: zakat, interest-free banking and sukuk. Taqi Usmani, a jurist long involved in the sharia supervision of Islamic banks, is the central figure there.

Recent Yaqeen and Ummatics papers deal with waqf, charity and the effects of neoliberalism.
