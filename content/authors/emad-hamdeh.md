Emad Hamdeh is Associate Professor of Arabic and Islamic Studies in the Humanities and Communication department of Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University (Daytona Beach). The Centre for Islamic Knowledge lists his degrees as an MA in Islamic Studies and Christian-Muslim Relations from Hartford Seminary and an MPhil and PhD in Theology from the University of Exeter. A 2014 conference abstract lists him at Montclair State University, so he has changed institutions; no birth date or place, and no appointment dates, were found in the pages this archive checked, so none are given.

His research is on Salafi thought, Islamic jurisprudence and hadith studies. His main academic work is on Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albānī and on the disputes between Salafi and traditionalist scholars over authority in Islamic law. His book *Salafism and Traditionalism: Scholarly Authority in Modern Islam* (Cambridge University Press, 2021) is built on that research, and two of his journal articles on al-Albānī (2016 and 2017) are linked from this archive under al-Albānī as writing about him. He has also written the Oxford Bibliographies entry on al-Albānī and the entry on his legal thought in the *Oxford Encyclopedia of Islam and Law*, and edited a special issue on Salafism.

His first book, *The Necessity of Hadith in Islam* (International Islamic Publishing House, 2011), argues that the Sunna is required to understand the Qur'an. He has since written papers for the Yaqeen Institute on hadith, the madhhabs and the *mawālī* (freed slaves and non-Arabs) in Islamic scholarship, and academic pieces on the "Qur'an-only" movement and on how the internet has challenged the authority of the *ʿulamāʾ* ("Shaykh Google").

Most of his academic articles are paywalled. This archive links to the free Yaqeen papers, to the open-access copy of his 2019 paper on hadith, and to the publisher pages for the rest.
