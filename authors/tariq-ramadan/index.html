Tariq Ramadan

طارق رمضان

b. 1962 · Switzerland / Egypt / Europe · Islamic studies scholar, philosopher, writer on European Islam and Islamic ethics

Tariq Ramadan was born in Geneva in 1962. His father, Saʿid Ramadan, was a leader of the Muslim Brotherhood in exile, and his maternal grandfather was Hasan al-Banna, the movement’s founder. He studied at the University of Geneva, taking a master’s in French literature and a doctorate in Arabic and Islamic studies, and he also studied classical Islamic sciences in Cairo.

He became widely known for arguing that Muslims in Europe and North America should see themselves as full citizens, not as a minority apart. In books such as To Be a European Muslim (1999) and Western Muslims and the Future of Islam (2004) he argued for a “Western Muslim” identity rooted in the sources of Islam and in the societies where Muslims live. His Radical Reform: Islamic Ethics and Liberation (2009) called for reform that begins with the interpretation of ethics. He was a visiting fellow at St Antony’s College, Oxford, from 2005 and Professor of Contemporary Islamic Studies there from 2009, and from 2012 he directed the Research Centre for Islamic Legislation and Ethics (CILE), launched that year in Doha at Qatar Foundation’s Education City. He lectured widely in Europe, North America and the Muslim world. His public positions were often debated, and in 2004 the United States revoked his visa before he could take up a post at Notre Dame.

His writing began in French and much of it has been translated. His earlier books, including Les musulmans dans la laïcité (1994) and his study of Islamic reformism from al-Afghani to Hasan al-Banna, Aux sources du renouveau musulman (1998), were written for French-speaking readers; later ones such as In the Footsteps of the Prophet (2007), The Quest for Meaning (2010) and Islam and the Arab Awakening (2012) appeared in English, and he has also published dialogue books, among them two with the philosopher Edgar Morin. Beyond his books he wrote essays and op-eds for newspapers and web outlets, a number of journal articles and book chapters on Islamic ethics, bioethics, sharia and European Muslims, and gave interviews to major broadcasters and publications. His lectures include courses on the Prophet’s life, Islamic ethics, Qur’anic commentary and his “Chronicles of Ramadan”, and many are online in series on his official YouTube channel and on the sites of Oxford and the CILE.

From October 2017 he was accused of rape and sexual assault by several women, and he took leave from Oxford in November 2017. In Switzerland, a Geneva appeals court convicted him of rape and sexual coercion in September 2024, and the conviction became final in 2025. In France he was tried in his absence and convicted in March 2026, a judgment that may be appealed. He has denied the allegations, though in 2018 he acknowledged consensual relations with some of the women. This archive lists his writings as intellectual works and does not assess those proceedings.

His books remain in copyright. His own website, tariqramadan.com, no longer works, so for each book, article, interview and lecture series the archive links to the best free copy it could find (for several books, a borrow-only scan on the Internet Archive) or, where there is none, to a catalogue page. A few entries are marked as unchecked where a record was found but the text could not be opened.
