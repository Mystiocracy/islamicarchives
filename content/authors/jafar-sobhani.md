Ja'far Sobhani is an Iranian Shia jurist and theologian based in Qom. After studying Arabic literature and legal theory in a seminary elsewhere in Iran, he moved to the Qom seminary in 1946, where he attended classes in jurisprudence, legal theory, Qur'anic exegesis and philosophy under teachers including Hossein Borujerdi, Ruhollah Khomeini and Mohammad Tabatabai.

He founded the Imam Sadiq Institute in Qom, ran the journals Maktab-e Islam and Kalam-e Islami, and is credited with building up the study of kalam (theology) in the Qom seminary. He also took part in drafting the constitution of the Islamic Republic.

Sobhani is a prolific author with hundreds of research works in Persian and Arabic, including multi-volume thematic and consecutive commentaries on the Qur'an and an eight-volume study of religions and sects. English readers know him mainly through Doctrines of Shi'i Islam, a compendium of Imami belief and practice translated by Reza Shah-Kazemi, and through translations of his biography of the Prophet and other works.
