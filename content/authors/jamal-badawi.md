Jamal Badawi is an Egyptian-born Canadian writer and speaker on Islam. He studied at Ain Shams University in Cairo, moved to the United States in the 1960s, and took a master's degree and doctorate in business administration at Indiana University Bloomington. He later taught management at the Sobey School of Business, Saint Mary's University, in Halifax, Nova Scotia, and has served as a volunteer imam of the Halifax Muslim community since 1970.

He founded and chairs the Islamic Information Foundation, which produces introductory material on Islam for non-Muslims, and he designed and presented a television series of several hundred half-hour episodes that was broadcast in Canada, the United States and elsewhere. He has been active in the Fiqh Council of North America, the European Council for Fatwa and Research and other bodies, and in 1997 he debated the Christian philosopher William Lane Craig on the concept of God.

His publications are mostly short books and pamphlets for general readers: on the status of women, gender equity, women's dress, polygamy, the prophethood of Muhammad and Islamic leadership. They present a modern, scripture-based defence of Islamic teaching and have been reproduced by Islamic organisations and websites.
