Shiblī Nuʿmānī

شبلی نعمانی

1857–1914 · British India · Historian, theologian, educationist, biographer

Shiblī Nuʿmānī was born in 1857 at Bandwal, near Azamgarh in the North-Western Provinces, to a landed family. He received a traditional madrasa education in Arabic, Persian, logic, hadith and fiqh under several teachers, among them Muhammad Faruq Chirayakoti, and adopted the name Nuʿmānī out of devotion to Abu Hanifa (al-Nuʿman ibn Thabit).

In 1882 he joined the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh, where he taught Arabic and Persian for sixteen years and came to know Sayyid Ahmad Khan and the orientalist Thomas Arnold. With Arnold he travelled in 1892 through Istanbul, Syria and Egypt, an experience recorded in his Safarnama-e-Rum-o-Misr-o-Sham. After 1898 he served as an adviser on education in Hyderabad, and from 1905 to 1913 he directed the Nadwat al-‘Ulama’ in Lucknow, where he tried to join the traditional curriculum to modern subjects. Opposition from conservative scholars forced him out, and he retired to Azamgarh, where he planned a research academy, the Dar al-Musannifin, that his pupil Sulaiman Nadvi opened days after his death in November 1914.

Shibli set out to give Muslims a modern, critical history of their own past. His biographies of the caliphs al-Ma’mun and ‘Umar (Al-Faruq), of Abu Hanifa, al-Ghazali and Rumi, and above all his unfinished Sirat al-Nabi, combined classical sources with the methods of European historiography while answering orientalist criticism. In ‘Ilm al-Kalam and Al-Kalam he called for a new theology able to meet modern objections. His Arabic Al-Intiqad rebutted Jurji Zaydan’s history of Islamic civilisation.

He was also a poet in Persian and Urdu and a founder of Urdu literary criticism. Through Nadwa, the Dar al-Musannifin and its journal Ma’arif, his combination of traditional scholarship and historical method shaped a whole school of South Asian Muslim writers.
