Muhammad Sa'id Ramadan al-Bouti was born in 1929 in the village of Jilka near Cizre, in what is now Turkey, to a Kurdish scholarly family, and was brought as a child to Damascus, where his father, Mulla Ramadan al-Bouti, taught. He studied the Qur'an, the Prophet's biography, tafsir, logic, rhetoric and legal theory in Damascus, then went to Cairo in 1954 to study at the Shari'a faculty of al-Azhar, where he also took a law degree and, in 1965, a doctorate.

He taught at the Shari'a faculty of Damascus University from 1960, served as its dean from 1977 to 1983, and later held a chair in comparative law. He was also a regular preacher and teacher at the Umayyad Mosque, to which he was formally appointed preacher in 2008, and a member of several international Islamic bodies. He was a prominent voice for Ash'ari theology, the Shafi'i school and the Sufi tradition, and a leading critic of the Salafi rejection of the legal schools and of Marxism and Western secular thought. His public support for the Syrian government during the uprising of 2011 was controversial.

He wrote around sixty books in Arabic, on theology and the existence of God, the Prophet's life, Islamic law, women, jihad, economics and Sufi devotion. He was killed on 21 March 2013 in a bomb attack on the al-Iman mosque in Damascus while teaching; responsibility for the attack remains disputed.
