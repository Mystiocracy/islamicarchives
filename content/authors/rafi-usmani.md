Muhammad Rafi Usmani was born in Deoband in 1936, the son of Mufti Muhammad Shafi, later Grand Mufti of Pakistan, and the elder brother of Taqi Usmani. His family moved to Pakistan in 1948. He memorised the Qur'an, entered Darul Uloom Karachi in 1951 and graduated from the traditional course in 1960, specialising afterwards in the issuing of legal opinions. He also sat Punjab University examinations in Arabic and Urdu.

He taught at Darul Uloom Karachi for decades, covering hadith and fiqh, and in 1986 succeeded Abdul Hai Arifi as its president. He sat on the Council of Islamic Ideology and other official bodies, advised Pakistan's Shariat Appellate Bench, and served as a vice-president of the Wifaq-ul-Madaris board. In the late 1980s he took part in the Afghan war and later published accounts of it. He died in Karachi in November 2022.

He wrote some two dozen books in Urdu and Arabic. They include a biography of his father, a memoir of the Afghan jihad (Yeh Tere Pur Asrar Bande), a study on the place of consensus in fiqh, works on women's leadership, economics and the signs of the Last Day, and travel and pilgrimage guides. His Urdu commentary on a classic text on the rules of Arabic verb forms is used in Deobandi curricula.
