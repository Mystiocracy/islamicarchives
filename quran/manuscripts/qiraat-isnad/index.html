The first seven were grouped by Ibn Mujāhid (d. 324) and versified by al-Shāṭibī (d. 590) in the Shāṭibiyya. Ibn al-Jazarī (d. 833) added the last three in al-Durra al-muḍiyya and set out all ten, with their ṭuruq, in al-Nashr and the Ṭayyibat al-Nashr. Below the ṭuruq shown here, al-Nashr branches into about 980 paths through particular books and teachers, which this chart does not attempt. Two people appear in two roles: Khalaf b. Hishām is Ḥamza's rāwī and the tenth Imam, and al-Dūrī narrates both Abū ʿAmr and al-Kisāʾī.

The Companions behind the readings

Most chains of the ten readings run back to a small group of Companions. Ubayy b. Kaʿb, Zayd b. Thābit, ʿUthmān, ʿAlī and Ibn Masʿūd stand behind almost all of them, with ʿUmar, Abū al-Dardāʾ and Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī in some chains. Zayd b. Thābit (d. 45) wrote down the revelation for the Prophet ﷺ, gathered the Qurʾān into one collection under Abū Bakr and led the committee that made ʿUthmān's copies; his students include Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī and Abū al-ʿĀliya, through whom his reading reaches Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim and the readings that descend from them.

How this chart was checked

Each reading was checked against its section of al-Nashr (Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyya edition, vol. 1, read on IslamWeb) and, for teachers, students and death dates, against Ghāyat al-nihāya (Bergsträsser's edition, cited as GN volume/page). Where the two disagree on a date, the chart gives the date Ibn al-Jazarī prefers and notes the alternative.
