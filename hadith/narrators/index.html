Narrators of the Ḥadīth

The Sanadset 650K dataset as a chart: 491,428 chains and 184,811 written narrator names from 953 books, with the text of every ḥadīth. Archived 24 October 2026.

This page shows Sanadset 650K and nothing else. The chains were extracted from the books by the dataset's authors, by program, and may contain mistakes. Names are shown as written in the chains, so one person can appear under several spellings; where a name looks like another's, the page says so but never joins them. English spellings are machine transliterations and the Arabic is the reference.

The chart starts at the Prophet ﷺ. Each narrator has teachers on the left and students on the right; the bars are as wide as the number of chains. Click a name to move to that narrator. You can limit the chart to chosen books (any number), and to chains that contain chosen narrators, in any order or in a set order, then open the ḥadīth behind any narrator or link.

      Reading the atlas

      • A chain is the list of narrators Sanadset records for one report, from the one nearest the compiler of the book back to the one nearest the Prophet ﷺ. Sanadset does not list the Prophet ﷺ as a narrator, so the chart shows him as the end of every chain. Some reports stop at a Companion or a later person; the dataset does not mark which.
      • Teachers and students. The teacher of a narrator is the next name toward the Prophet ﷺ in the chain; the student is the previous one. Every link seen in a chain is counted, even once. There is no minimum.
      • Counting. A chain counts once for a name even if the name is repeated in it. A ḥadīth that appears in several books is counted in each book.
      • Names. Names are exactly as written in the chains. To count them, vowel marks and tatweel are ignored and the alif forms (أ إ آ ٱ) are treated as one letter; nothing else is changed. Expressions such as “his father” (أبيه) are kept as written; they stand for different people in different chains.
      • Books are chosen with any number of ticks. A chain is kept if it is from any of the chosen books. The Six Books button chooses Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, Sunan al-Nasāʾī (al-Ṣughrā) and Sunan Ibn Māja.
      • Narrators. Chains must contain all the narrators you add. With “in this order”, the first name added is the one nearest the Prophet ﷺ and each later name must come after it, nearer the compiler.
      • Typical link from the end is the average distance of the name from the end of its chains (1 is the last name before the Prophet ﷺ). It is worked out from the chains and is not a ṭabaqa.
      • “May be the same person” is a hint, not data. Two written names are linked when they are the same once case endings, hamza and ya forms, Abū/Abī/Abā, ibn/bin and the article are ignored, or when one is a run of words inside the other (الزهري inside محمد بن مسلم بن شهاب الزهري) and the two have teachers and students in common. The score is how much they overlap, from 0 to 1. Names are never merged and their counts stay apart. A short name linked to several different longer names is marked as possibly several people.
      • English spellings are made by rules from the Arabic. Each word is spelled from its most common vowelled form found in Sanadset; a word that never appears vowelled gets a rough guess. Treat them as an aid for reading and searching; the Arabic is the reference.
      • Reports without a chain. 159,558 of the 650,986 reports in Sanadset are marked as having no chain and are not shown.
