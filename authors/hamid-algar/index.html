Hamid Algar

b. 1940 · United States (born in England) · Historian of Iran and Islam, professor emeritus of Persian studies at UC Berkeley

Hamid Algar was born in England in 1940 and read Oriental languages (Arabic and Persian) at Trinity College, Cambridge, graduating in 1961. He then studied in Tehran and Istanbul and completed a Cambridge doctorate in 1965 on the political role of the Shi’i clergy in nineteenth-century Iran. He embraced Islam as a young man.

He taught at the University of California, Berkeley from 1965 to 2010 and is professor emeritus of Persian studies there. His research covers Iranian Shi’ism, the Naqshbandi Sufi order and the intellectual history of the Persian, Turkish and Balkan Muslim worlds, and he has contributed many entries to Encyclopaedia Iranica.

He is also known as a translator of works by Ruhollah Khomeini, Ali Shariati and other twentieth-century Muslim thinkers. His own writing includes academic monographs, a study of Jami, and short books on topics such as the Qur’anic chapter al-Fatiha, the Sunna, Sufism and Wahhabism. His views on the Iranian revolution and some public remarks have drawn criticism.
