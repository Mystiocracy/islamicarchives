Ṭāhā ʿAbd al-Raḥmān was born in 1944 in El Jadida on Morocco's Atlantic coast, where he received his early schooling before attending secondary school in Casablanca. He studied philosophy at Mohammed V University in Rabat and then at the Sorbonne, where he completed a doctoral thesis in 1972 on the linguistic structures underlying ontology, published in French in 1979. A second doctorate, awarded in 1985, dealt with argumentation and natural reasoning. From 1970 until his retirement in 2005 he taught logic and the philosophy of language at Mohammed V University.

Trained in modern logic and analytic philosophy, he turned these tools toward the Arabic-Islamic intellectual heritage. *Fī uṣūl al-ḥiwār wa-tajdīd ʿilm al-kalām* (1987) treated theology as a form of dialogical argument, and *al-ʿAmal al-dīnī wa-tajdīd al-ʿaql* (1989) argued that reason is renewed through religious practice and spiritual experience, reflecting his own engagement with Sufism. In *Tajdīd al-manhaj fī taqwīm al-turāth* (1994) he criticised fragmentary, ideological readings of the heritage and called for an integrative approach.

From 2000 onward his work focused on ethics and modernity. *Suʾāl al-akhlāq* (2000) offered an ethical critique of Western modernity, while *Rūḥ al-ḥadātha* (2006) distinguished the universal spirit of modernity from its particular Western realisation and proposed the grounds for an Islamic modernity. In later books such as *Rūḥ al-dīn* (2012), *Buʾs al-dahrāniyya* (2014) and the three-volume *Dīn al-ḥayāʾ* (2017) he developed a "fiduciary" or trusteeship paradigm (*al-iʾtimāniyya*), which grounds ethics in the human being's trust from God and rejects the separation of morality from religion.

He has received the ISESCO Prize for Islamic thought and philosophy (2006) and other honours. His demanding style and coinages have attracted a large following among Arab students of philosophy, and his project is widely discussed as one of the most systematic contemporary attempts to rebuild Islamic ethics on philosophical foundations.
