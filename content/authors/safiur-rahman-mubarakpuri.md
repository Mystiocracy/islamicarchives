Safiur Rahman Mubarakpuri was born in 1942 near Mubarakpur in the Azamgarh district of Uttar Pradesh and studied at a series of madrasas in eastern Uttar Pradesh, finishing at Jamia Faiz-e-Aam in Mau in the early 1960s. He belonged to the Ahl-i Hadith current. For nearly three decades he taught in Indian schools and was principal of two madrasas. From 1974 to 1988 he was a teacher at Jamia Salafiyya in Varanasi, where he also edited its Urdu monthly Muhaddith.

In 1988 he moved to Saudi Arabia and worked as a researcher at a centre for the study of the Prophet's biography and Sunna attached to the Islamic University of Madinah. After 1997 he was based in Riyadh, working as a researcher for the publisher Darussalam.

He wrote in Arabic and Urdu. His best-known book, Ar-Raheeq al-Makhtum, is a one-volume biography of the Prophet that won a prize at a Muslim World League competition held in the late 1970s and has been translated widely, including into English as The Sealed Nectar. His other works include a further biography, When the Moon Split, an Arabic commentary on Sahih Muslim, and Urdu writings on hadith and on the Ahmadiyya movement. He died in his home village in December 2006.
