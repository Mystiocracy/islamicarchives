Omid Safi was born in Jacksonville, Florida, and raised in Iran, moving with his family from Tehran to the United States in 1985. He is a professor of Asian and Middle Eastern studies at Duke University, where he directed the Duke Islamic Studies Center from 2014 to 2019, and was earlier on the faculty of the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

His scholarship ranges across medieval Persian Islam, the history of the Prophet's memory, and Sufism. He is associated with the progressive Muslim movement, which emphasises social justice, gender equality and pluralism, and he edited the volume Progressive Muslims. He has written a column for On Being and writes widely for general readers.

His own books include a study of ideology and religious inquiry in the Seljuq period, a historical account of the figure of Muhammad in Muslim memory, and an anthology of teachings on love from the Islamic mystical tradition.
