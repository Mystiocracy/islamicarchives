Ahmed Hoosen Deedat was born on 1 July 1918 in Tadkeshwar, in the Bombay Presidency of British India, to a Gujarati Muslim family. His father had already emigrated to South Africa, and Deedat joined him there at the age of nine. He worked as a salesman, and an encounter with Christian missionaries in 1936 pushed him to study the Bible and works such as Rahmatullah Kairanawi's *Izhar ul-Haqq*. He was never trained as a formal religious scholar.

He gave his first public lecture in Durban in 1942 and gradually became known for public lectures and debates on the relations between Islam and Christianity. In 1957 he and two friends founded the Islamic Propagation Centre International (IPCI), and in 1958 he established the As-Salaam educational institute near Durban. His international profile grew in the 1980s, and he received the King Faisal International Prize for service to Islam in 1986. A stroke in 1996 left him paralysed and unable to speak, and he died in Verulam, KwaZulu-Natal, on 8 August 2005.

His writing consists of short booklets, mostly from the IPCI, on the Bible, the crucifixion, the person of Jesus, the Qur'an and the Prophet Muhammad, several of which were later gathered in volumes such as *The Choice*. They are polemical in tone and designed for popular distribution, and his style and methods have drawn criticism from many commentators as well as admiration from others.
