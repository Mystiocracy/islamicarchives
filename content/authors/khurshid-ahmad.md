Khurshid Ahmad was born in Delhi in 1932 and moved to Pakistan after the partition of India, settling first in Lahore and later in Karachi. He studied economics at Government College Lahore and the University of Karachi, took an MA in Islamic studies, and completed a doctorate in economics at the University of Leicester in the late 1960s. From his student days he was an activist in the Jamaat-e-Islami and a follower of Abul A'la Maududi.

In England he was one of the founders of the Islamic Foundation in Leicester, a research and publishing centre, and he was closely associated with the development of Islamic economics as an academic field. In Pakistan he served as an adviser on economic planning in the 1980s, and was a senator from 2002 to 2012 for the Muttahida Majlis-e-Amal. He received the King Faisal Award for Service to Islam in 1990 and Pakistan's Nishan-e-Imtiaz in 2011.

His own writing, in English and Urdu, consists mostly of short books and essays on Islam and the West, Islamic education, family life, the Pakistani economy and the Islamic movement. He also translated Maududi and edited several collective volumes and conference proceedings, which are not listed here. He died in Leicester in April 2025.
