Mohammad Akram Nadwi

محمد أكرم الندوي

United Kingdom; India · Hadith scholar and jurist, author of the biographical dictionary of women hadith scholars

Mohammad Akram Nadwi was born in Jaunpur district, Uttar Pradesh, and trained at the Darul Uloom Nadwatul Ulama in Lucknow, where one of his teachers was Abul Hasan Ali Nadwi. He studied there and later at the University of Lucknow. He later settled in Britain, where he was a research fellow at the University of Oxford, and he now leads the Al-Salam Institute and its research centre and is associated with Cambridge Islamic College and the Markfield Institute of Higher Education.

His writing is in Arabic, English and Urdu, and covers hadith, fiqh, biography, travel and the teaching of Arabic grammar and the Islamic sciences. His best-known work is the Arabic biographical dictionary Al-Wafa bi-Asma al-Nisa, published in forty-three volumes in 2021, which records thousands of women who transmitted or taught hadith; an English introduction to it appeared earlier as Al-Muhaddithat: The Women Scholars in Islam. He has also written a biography of Abu Hanifa and a Hanafi manual of ritual law in English, as well as studies of modern Indian scholars such as Sulayman Nadwi and Shibli Nomani.

He follows the Hanafi school while arguing for reasoned, text-based legal judgment, and the journalist Carla Power described a year of study with him in her book If the Oceans Were Ink.
