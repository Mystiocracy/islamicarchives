Bakr ibn 'Abd Allah Abu Zayd was a Saudi jurist and writer in the Salafi tradition, with a strong interest in Hanbali law, born around 1944. After early schooling he moved to Riyadh in 1955 and went on to the Shari'a faculty of Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University, graduating in 1966. He later completed a master's degree and, in 1982, a doctorate at the Higher Judicial Institute. He is recorded as a student of Muhammad al-Amin al-Shinqiti.

His career combined library work, the judiciary and public office: he worked in the library of the Islamic University of Madinah from 1964, was appointed a judge in 1966, and in 1979 was appointed to a senior legal post in the Ministry of Justice. From 1985 until his death he was president of the International Islamic Fiqh Academy, and in 1991 he joined Saudi Arabia's Council of Senior Scholars and its Permanent Committee for Islamic Research and Fatwas.

He wrote dozens of short, tightly argued books and treatises. Many are on the contemporary application of Islamic law (including a study of new legal problems, Fiqh al-nawazil), on hadith methodology, on the biography and legal thought of Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya and on the Hanbali scholars, and on manners and conduct for students and writers, of which his Hilyat talib al-'ilm is the best known. He died in February 2008 and was buried in Diriyah.
