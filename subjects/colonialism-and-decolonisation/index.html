Colonialism and decolonisation

Muslim thought under empire and after independence.

Writing under empire often begins by trying to explain defeat: Sayyid Ahmad Khan on the 1857 revolt in India, al-Afghani and Abduh from Paris. Later authors turn to what colonial rule did to Muslim ways of thinking. Malek Bennabi used the word 'colonisability' for the inner condition that lets a society be ruled, and Muhammad Asad, Maryam Jameelah and Wael Hallaq take on the West's picture of Islam and its education and legal systems.

Papers from the Yaqeen and Ummatics institutes carry the discussion to the present, with colonial law, digital colonialism and the afterlife of imperial categories.

    The Causes of the Indian Revolt ↗ Sayyid Ahmad Khan

    A loyal Indian Muslim's analysis of 1857 that places much of the blame on British misgovernment.

  2. 1884 · Collection
    The Firmest Bond ↗ Jamāl al-Dīn al-Afghānī

    The Paris weekly of al-Afghani and Abduh, calling for Muslim unity and resistance to European control.

  3. 1914 · Book
    With the Turk in Wartime ↗ Muhammad Marmaduke Pickthall

    An English convert's account of Istanbul in 1913, sympathetic to the Ottomans.

  4. 1934 · Book
    Islam at the Crossroads ↗ Muhammad Asad

    Asad's early critique of Western civilisation and of Muslim imitation of it.

  5. 1958 · Book
    The Ideological Struggle in Colonised Countries ↗ Malek Bennabi

    Bennabi on how the colonial period shaped ideas as well as institutions.

  6. 1962 · Book
    Islam versus the West ↗ Maryam Jameelah

    Maryam Jameelah's argument against Westernisation, written from experience of both worlds.

  7. 2018 · Book
    Restating Orientalism: A Critique of Modern Knowledge Wael Hallaq

    Hallaq on how the modern academy inherited the assumptions of Orientalism.
