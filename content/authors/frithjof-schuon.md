Frithjof Schuon was born in Basel in 1907 to parents of German origin and became a French citizen after the family moved to Mulhouse. He worked as a textile designer while reading the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita and, above all, the work of Rene Guenon, with whom he later corresponded for years. He was initiated into the Alawi Sufi order in Algeria, and in the later 1930s he began to act as a spiritual guide himself, eventually leading to the independent Maryamiyya order. He lived in Lausanne from the early 1940s and, from 1980, in Bloomington, Indiana.

Writing in French (and, late in life, poetry in German), he published more than twenty books on metaphysics, comparative religion, sacred art and spiritual practice. His central thesis is that the world's revealed religions differ in form but share one transcendent truth, so that each must be practised in its own tradition. Several of his books deal directly with Islam and Sufism, notably Understanding Islam, Sufism: Veil and Quintessence and Christianity/Islam.

He is a founding figure of the Traditionalist or Perennialist school and influenced writers such as Martin Lings. His later life was marked by controversy: allegations made against him in 1991 were dropped by prosecutors but caused some followers to leave his order. He died in 1998.
