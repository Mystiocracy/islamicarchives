Browse by subject

16 subjects, each with a reading path to begin with.

All subjects →
  1. 357 Hadith studies Modern writing on hadith keeps returning to two questions: how reliable are the reports attributed to the Prophet, and who has the right to say so. 357 works
  2. 424 Islamic law This section collects what modern authors say about fiqh and its sources: how the schools worked, what happened to Islamic law under colonial rule and state codification, and whether it can be revived, reformed or has to be rethought. 424 works
  3. 1217 Islam and modernity This is the question that most of the archive is about. 1217 works
  4. 80 Colonialism and decolonisation Writing under empire often begins by trying to explain defeat: Sayyid Ahmad Khan on the 1857 revolt in India, al-Afghani and Abduh from Paris. 80 works
  5. 120 Women and gender Writing on women ranges from handbooks on rights and dress to historical studies of how patriarchal readings settled and new readings of the Qur'an itself. 120 works
  6. 718 Philosophy and theology Theology here means kalām, doctrine (ʿaqīda) and metaphysics as modern authors reread them. 718 works
  7. 779 Political thought For most of this section the starting point is the end of the caliphate, abolished in 1924. 779 works
  8. 342 Sufism Authors come to Sufism from several directions. 342 works
  9. 109 Economics Islamic economics took shape as a discipline in the 1940s, partly as an answer to socialism and capitalism. 109 works
  10. 26 Slavery and race This section follows how Muslim scholars have dealt with slavery in law and history and with race and anti-Blackness in the community. 26 works
  11. 368 Education From al-Tahtawi's mission to Paris in the 1820s to the madrasa reforms of the late nineteenth century, Muslim writers have returned to schools: what to teach, in what language, and who should run them. 368 works
  12. 526 Qurʾānic studies Three kinds of work sit here. 526 works
  13. 106 Psychology and mental health Most of the entries are papers on mental health from the Yaqeen Institute, dealing with anxiety, shame, doubt and trauma. 106 works
  14. 122 Science and religion Early authors such as al-Afghani, Abduh and Sayyid Ahmad Khan argued over whether Islam is compatible with modern science and how far the Qur'an should be read in its light. 122 works
  15. 346 Family and society Family law is covered under Islamic law and gender under women and gender. 346 works
  16. 158 Sira and history Biographies of the Prophet are the biggest part of this section, written for different audiences: Shibli Nomani's Sirat al-Nabi (completed after his death by Sulayman Nadwi), Martin Lings's Muhammad, Hamidullah's edition of Ibn Ishaq, and Nasr's Muhammad: Man of Allah. 158 works
