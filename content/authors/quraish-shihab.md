Muhammad Quraish Shihab was born in 1944 in Rappang, South Sulawesi, into an Arab-Indonesian family of Ba 'Alawi descent; his father, Abdurrahman Shihab, was himself a scholar and university professor. After schooling in Makassar and an Islamic boarding school in Malang he went to Cairo in 1958 and studied at al-Azhar, where he took a bachelor's degree in tafsir and hadith in 1967, a master's in Qur'anic exegesis in 1969, and in 1982 a doctorate in Qur'anic sciences with a dissertation on the commentary of the fifteenth-century scholar al-Biqa'i.

He taught in Makassar and, from 1984, at the State Islamic Institute (now university) in Jakarta, and has held posts with the Indonesian Ulema Council, the Qur'an Review Board of the Ministry of Religious Affairs and the national education advisory board. He served briefly as Indonesia's Minister of Religious Affairs in 1998 and then as ambassador to Egypt from 1999 to 2002.

Shihab writes in Indonesian for a broad readership. His best-known work is Tafsir al-Mishbah, a complete Qur'an commentary in fifteen volumes, which shares its name with a television programme he has presented. Other books treat the methods and themes of the Qur'an, answers to everyday religious questions, and contested topics such as dress and Sunni-Shia relations. Some of his rulings, for example on greeting Christians at Christmas and on the obligation of the headscarf, have been controversial in Indonesia.
