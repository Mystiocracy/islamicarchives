Habib Ali al-Jifri (full name 'Ali Zayn al-'Abidin al-Jifri) was born in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, on 16 April 1971. He is a Yemeni Sunni scholar, preacher and spiritual teacher, who is honoured as a sayyid, a descendant of the Prophet through his grandson al-Husayn, and who is based mainly in Cairo.

He founded the Tabah Foundation, a research and educational institute in Abu Dhabi, and has been a regular participant in Muslim-Christian and interfaith dialogue. He has appeared on the annual list of the world's most influential Muslims compiled at Georgetown University since 2009 (ranked 22nd in 2023), and in 2008 received the Eugen Biser Award for his part in the "A Common Word" initiative.

His writing is addressed to a general Muslim readership and to interfaith audiences. Among his books are The Concept of Faith in Islam, a paper presented to the Catholic-Muslim Forum and issued in English and Arabic in 2012 by the Royal Islamic Strategic Studies Centre in Amman, and al-Insaniyya qabl al-tadayyun ("Humanity before Religiosity"), a book on intellectual problems and the challenges facing contemporary Islamic discourse and young people.
