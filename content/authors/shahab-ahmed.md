Shahab Ahmed was born in Singapore in 1966 to Pakistani parents, both physicians. After schooling in Singapore and at Caterham School in England, he took a first degree at the International Islamic University Malaysia and a master's degree at the American University in Cairo, and worked for a period as a journalist in Afghanistan before completing a doctorate at Princeton.

He was a Junior Fellow of the Harvard Society of Fellows from 2000 to 2003, taught at Princeton, and from 2005 was associate professor of Islamic studies at Harvard; he also spent a year as a visiting scholar at the Islamic Research Institute in Islamabad and later held a research post at Harvard Law School. He was noted for reading sources in a very large number of languages.

His research centred on Islamic intellectual history. His dissertation and early articles examined the so-called Satanic verses, reports that the Prophet once recited lines honouring Meccan goddesses, and how Muslim scholars judged their reliability; *Before Orthodoxy* (2017) argues that early authorities largely accepted the story and that its rejection was a later product of orthodoxy. He also wrote on Ottoman madrasa curricula and Central Asian scholarship, and contributed an overview of hadith to the *Encyclopaedia Iranica*.

His major work, *What Is Islam? The Importance of Being Islamic* (2015), challenges definitions of Islam that make law or scripture its sole measure. Drawing on the philosophical, Sufi, poetic and artistic culture of the region he called the "Balkans-to-Bengal complex", he proposed understanding Islam as human engagement with revelation as Pre-Text, Text and Con-Text, an approach that has been widely discussed. Ahmed died of leukaemia in September 2015, aged 48, shortly before the book appeared; *Before Orthodoxy* was published after his death.
