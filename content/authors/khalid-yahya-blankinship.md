Khalid Yahya Blankinship was born in Seattle in 1949. He took a history degree at the University of Washington in 1973, a master's in teaching English as a foreign language at the American University in Cairo in 1975, a master's in Islamic history at Cairo University in 1983, and a doctorate in history at the University of Washington in 1988. He is a professor in the Department of Religion at Temple University in Philadelphia.

His research covers early Islamic history, the Umayyad caliphate, the Qur'an and its rhetoric, religion and science, and the ethics of war. He translated and annotated two volumes of al-Tabari's *History* (volumes 11 and 25), a standard source for the early conquests and for the Umayyad period.

His principal monograph, *The End of the Jihad State* (1994), reassesses the reign of the caliph Hisham ibn 'Abd al-Malik and argues that military and logistical limits, rather than internal decay, ended the Umayyad expansion. In *The Inimitable Qur'an* he examines the Qur'an's rhetorical features and what is lost in English translations.
