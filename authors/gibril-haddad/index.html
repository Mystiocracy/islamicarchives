Gibril Haddad

جبريل فؤاد حداد

b. 1960 · Lebanon · Hadith scholar, author and translator of classical Islamic texts

Gibril Fouad Haddad was born in Beirut in 1960 into a Lebanese Catholic family and was schooled by Jesuits. The civil war forced his family out of Lebanon in the 1970s; he finished school in Britain, then studied at Columbia College in New York and began graduate work in French literature at Columbia University. He has described his move to Islam as the end of a long spell of reading and listening, and he took the shahada in 1991.

Soon afterwards he met Hisham Kabbani and, through him, Shaykh Nazim al-Haqqani, whom he accepted as his guide in the Naqshbandi Sufi order. He then spent roughly a decade in Damascus studying with a range of Syrian scholars, and also took instruction in Mecca, Morocco and Beirut. He began as a Hanafi and later adopted the Shafi’i school. He reports holding authorisations (ijazas) from well over a hundred teachers.

His writing consists largely of translations of classical Arabic works on creed and hadith, together with a smaller body of his own books and studies. The latter include a polemical guide to the Salafi movement, a hajj journal and a survey of the founders of the four Sunni legal schools. From 2013 to 2018 he held posts at the Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien Centre for Islamic Studies at Universiti Brunei Darussalam, and he serves as chief editor of the Integrated Encyclopedia of the Qur’an. He was included in the first edition of the 500 Most Influential Muslims in 2009.
