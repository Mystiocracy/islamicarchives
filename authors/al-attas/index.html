Syed Muhammad Naquib al-Attas

سيد محمد نقيب العطاس

1931–2026 · Malaysia · Philosopher, metaphysician, historian of Malay Islam, educationist

Syed Muhammad Naquib al-Attas was born in 1931 in Bogor, Java, then part of the Dutch East Indies, into a family of Hadrami sayyid descent established in the Malay world; the sociologist Syed Hussein Alatas was his elder brother. He was schooled in Johor and Java, trained as an officer at the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst, and then left the army for academic study at the University of Malaya. He took an MA at McGill University’s Institute of Islamic Studies and a doctorate at the School of Oriental and African Studies in London with a thesis on the Sufi poet Hamzah Fansuri.

Returning to Malaysia, he headed Malay literature studies at the University of Malaya and played a leading part in founding the National University of Malaysia, where he held its first chair of Malay language and literature. His early works on Sufism in the Malay world, on al-Rānīrī and on the Islamisation of the archipelago argued that Islam, rather than Hindu-Buddhist culture, had shaped the rational and literary character of Malay civilisation.

From the 1970s al-Attas became a major voice in global Islamic thought. In Islam and Secularism (1978) he diagnosed the central problem of Muslims as a ‘loss of adab’ and a confusion of knowledge produced by Western secular philosophy, and he advanced the idea of the ‘Islamization of present-day knowledge’. The Concept of Education in Islam (1980) presented education as taʾdīb, the instilling of right order and adab. His Prolegomena to the Metaphysics of Islam (1995) gathered his writings on the Islamic worldview, being, knowledge and happiness, drawing heavily on Sufi metaphysics and classical kalām.

In 1987 he founded the International Institute of Islamic Thought and Civilization (ISTAC) in Kuala Lumpur, which he directed for many years. Honoured as a Royal Professor in Malaysia in 2024, he died in March 2026 at the age of 94; his students continue his work at centres in Malaysia and elsewhere.
