Allal al-Fassi

علال الفاسي

1910–1974 · Morocco · Nationalist leader, jurist and writer, leader of the Istiqlal Party

Allal al-Fassi was born in Fez in January 1910 into a scholarly urban family. He learned the Qur’an as a child and studied at the Qarawiyyin, where he fell in with students influenced by the Salafi reform movement. Politically conscious from the mid-1920s, he helped found a students’ union at the Qarawiyyin in 1927 and took a degree in Islamic law around 1930 to 1932, using his public lectures to promote reform and national awakening under the French protectorate.

After the unrest of 1937 he was sent into exile in Gabon, returning to Morocco in 1947. He was a leading figure of the Istiqlal (Independence) Party, founded in 1943, and later spent years in Cairo, where he taught at al-Azhar and worked with other North African nationalists. After independence he served as Morocco’s minister of Islamic affairs from 1961 to 1963, and he led Istiqlal until his death in Bucharest on 13 May 1974.

He was also a poet and a prolific writer in Arabic. His best-known book, al-Naqd al-dhātī (“Self-Criticism”, 1952), was a guide for a would-be independent Morocco; his history of the independence movements in the Arab Maghreb and his writings on the objectives of Islamic law (maqāṣid al-sharīʿa) place him among the main Moroccan thinkers of Islamic reform. He also argued for a wider “Greater Morocco”, a position that remains contested.
