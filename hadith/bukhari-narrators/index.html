Reading the tree

  • Colours show the generation (ṭabaqa) in Ibn Ḥajar's scheme in Taqrīb al-Tahdhīb: 1 for the Companions, 2 to 6 for the Successors from senior to junior, 7 to 9 for their followers, and 10 to 12 for the generations al-Bukhārī studied with.
  • The number after a name is how many of al-Bukhārī's ḥadīth pass along that link. Thicker lines carry more ḥadīth. Tap the name and the card lists those ḥadīth; tap one of them to light up its full chain in gold.
  • A dashed ring means the name has a further generation to open. Long lists open twelve at a time; tap “more” for the rest.
  • Dates are death years in hijri; the narrator card adds the Common Era year.
  • Grades follow muslimscholars.info's summary of Ibn Ḥajar's verdicts: thiqa (trustworthy), thiqa thiqa (doubly so), ṣadūq (truthful), maqbūl (acceptable when supported) and so on.

About the data

The chains come from the Hadith Narrators dataset, which gives the narrators of each ḥadīth in the Six Books as numbered entries from muslimscholars.info. For al-Bukhārī it gives chains for 7,181 of the 7,580 ḥadīth, which were matched to the sunnah.com numbering by comparing their Arabic text. The names, dates, generations and grades were then taken from each narrator's page on muslimscholars.info. A link between two people means one received a ḥadīth from the other in at least one of al-Bukhārī's chains.

Some limits follow from this. The dataset keeps one chain per ḥadīth, so many of al-Bukhārī's second and third chains for the same text are missing, as are most of his suspended reports (taʿlīq). Al-Bukhārī repeats ḥadīth under several chapters, and the counts include the repeats. Names in the chains were matched to people automatically, and a few links look doubtful (a Companion shown narrating from a later figure, for instance). Chains that end at a Companion are joined to the Prophet ﷺ; those that end at a later narrator are left as they are. Sixteen narrators have no generation recorded and about 330 have no death date.
