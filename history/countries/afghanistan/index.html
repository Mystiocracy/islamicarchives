History of

Afghanistan

9 periods · 661 – today. Each period below is a power that ruled all or part of what is now Afghanistan.

  1. 661–750

    Umayyad Caliphate

    Damascus

    The first hereditary dynasty. Its rule stretched from Spain and North Africa to Sindh and Central Asia. Arabic became the language of administration and the Dome of the Rock was built.

    Rulers: Muʿāwiya I (661–680) · ʿAbd al-Malik (685–705) · al-Walīd I (705–715) · ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz (717–720) · Hishām (724–743) · Marwān II (744–750)

    Further reading ↗

  2. 750–1258

    Abbasid Caliphate

    Baghdad

    The caliphate of the great age of translation, law, theology and science. Real power passed to regional sultans from the ninth century. The Mongols sacked Baghdad in 1258 and killed the last caliph there.

    Rulers: al-Saffāḥ (750–754) · al-Manṣūr (754–775) · Hārūn al-Rashīd (786–809) · al-Maʾmūn (813–833) · al-Muʿtaṣim (833–842) · al-Mutawakkil (847–861) · al-Mustaʿṣim (1242–1258)

    Further reading ↗

  3. 977–1186

    Ghaznavids

    Ghazna

    A Turkic dynasty in what is now Afghanistan. It ruled Khurasan and raided into northern India, which carried Islam deeper into the subcontinent.

    Rulers: Sebüktigin (977–997) · Maḥmūd of Ghazna (998–1030)

    Further reading ↗

  4. 1037–1194

    Great Seljuk Empire

    Nishapur, Isfahan, Baghdad

    Sunni Turkic sultans who took Baghdad in 1055 and ruled in the caliph’s name. Their vizier Niẓām al-Mulk founded the Niẓāmiyya madrasas.

    Rulers: Ṭughril Beg (1037–1063) · Alp Arslān (1063–1072) · Malik-Shāh I (1072–1092)

    Further reading ↗

  5. 1370–1507

    Timurid Empire

    Samarkand, Herat

    Founded by Tīmūr. Its courts were centres of Persian art, astronomy and poetry, and its descendants founded the Mughal dynasty.

    Rulers: Tīmūr (1370–1405) · Shāh Rukh (1405–1447) · Ulugh Beg (1447–1449) · Ḥusayn Bayqara (1469–1506)

    Further reading ↗

  6. 1501–1736

    Safavid Empirewest

    Tabriz, Qazvin, Isfahan

    Made Twelver Shiʿism the state religion of Iran. Its rule saw great philosophy, architecture and art in Isfahan.

    Rulers: Ismāʿīl I (1501–1524) · Ṭahmāsp I (1524–1576) · ʿAbbās I the Great (1588–1629) · Sulṭān Ḥusayn (1694–1722)

    Further reading ↗

  7. 1526–1857

    Mughal Empireparts

    Delhi, Agra

    Founded by Bābur. It ruled most of the Indian subcontinent and reached its height under Akbar and Shāh Jahān. Britain deposed the last emperor after the 1857 rebellion.

    Rulers: Bābur (1526–1530) · Humāyūn (1530–1556) · Akbar (1556–1605) · Jahāngīr (1605–1627) · Shāh Jahān (1628–1658) · Awrangzīb (1658–1707) · Bahādur Shāh II (1837–1857)

    Further reading ↗

  8. 1979–1999

    Revolution and renewal

    Tehran, Kabul, Sarajevo

    The Iranian revolution of 1979 founded the Islamic Republic. The Soviet war in Afghanistan, Islamisation programmes and new Muslim scholarship marked the period.

    Rulers: Ruhollah Khomeini (1979–1989)

  9. 2000–today

    Contemporary voices

    Worldwide

    Writing in the age of globalisation and the internet. The Arab uprisings began in 2010–11, and Muslim intellectuals now write for global audiences in many languages.
