<p class="first">Maryam Jameelah (1934–2012) was born Margaret Marcus in New York and grew up in a secular Jewish family. A lifelong reader of religion and history, she embraced Islam in 1961 and the following year emigrated to Pakistan at the invitation of Sayyid Abul A‘la Maududi, settling in Lahore, where she lived for the rest of her life.</p><p>She became one of the best-known Western-born writers of the Islamic revival, the author of some thirty books and many essays defending traditional Islam against secular modernism, including <i>Islam versus the West</i>, <i>Islam and Modernism</i> and <i>Islam in Theory and Practice</i>. Her correspondence with Maududi was later published as <i>Correspondence between Maulana Maudoodi and Maryam Jameelah</i>.</p>
