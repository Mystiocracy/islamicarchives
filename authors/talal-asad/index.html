Talal Asad

طلال أسد

b. 1932 · Saudi Arabia / Britain / United States · Anthropologist, theorist of religion, secularism and Islam as a discursive tradition

Talal Asad was born in 1932 in Medina, the son of the writer and Qurʾan translator Muhammad Asad (Leopold Weiss), and grew up in Pakistan. He studied architecture briefly, then anthropology at Edinburgh, and took his doctorate at Oxford in 1968 under E. E. Evans-Pritchard, with a thesis on the Kababish of Sudan. He taught at Khartoum and Hull, then in the United States at the New School for Social Research, Johns Hopkins and the Graduate Center of the City University of New York, where he is Distinguished Professor Emeritus of Anthropology.

His Genealogies of Religion (1993) criticised the idea that “religion” is a universal category defined by private belief, and showed how it grew out of Christian and modern European history. His essay “The Idea of an Anthropology of Islam” (1986) proposed that Islam be studied as a discursive tradition, in which practices and arguments are linked to the past and to a community’s debates about what is right. In Formations of the Secular (2003), On Suicide Bombing (2007) and later essays he examined secularism, the modern state, law, violence and human rights.

His writings remain in copyright. The archive links to the essays and papers that are freely available; his two major books, Genealogies of Religion and Formations of the Secular, exist online only as user uploads and are kept hidden until you decide.
