Mohammad Hassan Khalil is an American scholar of Islamic studies. He studied at the University of Michigan, where he earned his bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees. He taught religion and law at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign before joining Michigan State University as professor of religious studies, adjunct professor of law and director of its Muslim studies programme. He has served on the board of the Society for the Study of Muslim Ethics and has been an office-holder in the North American Association of Islamic and Muslim Studies.

His research centres on Islamic theology and on how Muslim thinkers have understood salvation and the fate of non-Muslims. *Islam and the Fate of Others: The Salvation Question* (2012) examines the views of classical and later scholars, including al-Ghazali, Ibn 'Arabi and Ibn Taymiyya, and he later edited *Between Heaven and Hell*, a volume of related essays.

In *Jihad, Radicalism, and the New Atheism*, he looks at the claims of both militant groups and polemical critics of Islam about what the tradition teaches on violence, and offers a reading drawn from the classical sources.
