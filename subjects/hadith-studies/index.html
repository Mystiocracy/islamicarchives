Hadith studies

The transmission, criticism and authority of the Prophet’s sayings and example.

Modern writing on hadith keeps returning to two questions: how reliable are the reports attributed to the Prophet, and who has the right to say so. Muslim scholars answered through the long tradition of isnād criticism, and many of the authors here defend, refine or update it. Western scholars from Ignaz Goldziher and Joseph Schacht onward argued that a good part of the corpus arose in later generations, and a large share of the modern literature is a reply to them.

The section also covers the practical questions: how to read a report in its context (al-Qaradawi), how hadith grading became a dispute between Salafi and traditional scholars (al-Albani, Abu Ghudda, Emad Hamdeh), and how the Qur'an-only position has been answered.

Where to begin

A reading order, earliest first. Each title opens the text or the free copy.

  1. 1984 · Book
    Lamahat min Tarikh al-Sunna wa-'Ulum al-Hadith ↗ Abd al-Fattah Abu Ghudda

    A short Arabic history of the hadith sciences by a Syrian scholar, a good place to see how the tradition describes itself.

  2. 2009 · Book
    Hadith: Muhammad's Legacy in the Medieval and Modern World ↗ Jonathan A. C. Brown

    A history of the hadith tradition from the first generations to the present, aimed at readers coming to it for the first time.

  3. 2014 · Book
    Misquoting Muhammad: The Challenge and Choices of Interpreting the Prophet's Legacy ↗ Jonathan A. C. Brown

    Brown on the problems of interpreting the Prophet's legacy today.

  4. 2019 · Paper
    Blind Spots: The Origins of the Western Method of Critiquing Hadith ↗ Jonathan A. C. Brown

    On the origins of the Western method of criticising hadith, and what it took for granted.

  5. 2021 · Book
    Salafism and Traditionalism: Scholarly Authority in Modern Islam ↗ Emad Hamdeh

    Follows the arguments between Salafi and traditional scholars over authority, with al-Albani at the centre.
