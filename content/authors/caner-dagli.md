Caner Dagli is an American scholar of Islamic thought. He studied Near Eastern studies at Cornell University, took a master's degree in religion at George Washington University, and completed a doctorate in Near Eastern studies at Princeton University. He has taught at Roanoke College and at the College of the Holy Cross in Massachusetts, and in 2006-2007 advised the Royal Hashemite Court of Jordan on interfaith affairs.

He is known for his work on Sufi metaphysics and Islamic intellectual history. He translated Ibn al-'Arabi's *Fusus al-Hikam* as *The Ringstones of Wisdom* (2004) and was a general editor, with Seyyed Hossein Nasr, Joseph Lumbard and Mohammed Rustom, of *The Study Quran* (2015), as well as an editor of a reference work on philosophy, science and technology in Islam.

His authored books are *Ibn al-'Arabi and Islamic Intellectual Culture* (2016), which traces how Ibn al-'Arabi's thought was received and absorbed into Islamic philosophy and theology, and *Metaphysical Institutions: Islam and the Modern Project* (2024), a study of the modern project from an Islamic metaphysical standpoint.
