Authors

Jonathan A. C. Brown

b. 1977 · United States · Scholar of hadith and Islamic law, historian

Jonathan A. C. Brown was born in Washington, D.C., in 1977 and raised in the Episcopal Church. He converted to Islam in 1997 while an undergraduate at Georgetown University, where he graduated in history in 2000. After a year of intensive Arabic at the Center for Arabic Study Abroad in Cairo, he completed a doctorate in Islamic thought at the University of Chicago in 2006.

Brown taught at the University of Washington from 2006 to 2010 and then returned to Georgetown’s School of Foreign Service, where he holds the Alwaleed bin Talal Chair of Islamic Civilization and for some years directed the Prince Alwaleed Bin Talal Center for Muslim-Christian Understanding. He served as editor-in-chief of the Oxford Encyclopedia of Islam and Law and has been associated with the Yaqeen Institute for Islamic Research. He has carried out research in many Muslim-majority countries and has been working on an English translation of Sahih al-Bukhari.

His scholarship centres on hadith and Islamic law. The Canonization of al-Bukhari and Muslim (2007) explained how two collections came to hold canonical status in Sunni Islam, and Hadith: Muhammad’s Legacy in the Medieval and Modern World (2009) became a widely used introduction to the field. Misquoting Muhammad (2014) explores how scholars have interpreted scripture and the difficulties modern Muslims face in doing so, while Slavery and Islam (2019) and Islam and Blackness (2022) address slavery, race and moral judgement across history. A 2026 book examines the mazalim courts and legal reform.

Writing as both an academic historian and a practising Sunni Muslim, Brown engages critically with Western historical-critical scholarship while presenting classical Muslim scholarship on its own terms, and his books reach both university and general Muslim readerships.
