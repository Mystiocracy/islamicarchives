Talal Asad was born in 1932 in Medina, the son of the writer and Qurʾan translator Muhammad Asad (Leopold Weiss), and grew up in Pakistan. He studied at Edinburgh and took his doctorate in anthropology at Oxford. He taught at Hull and Manchester, and then in the United States at the Graduate Center of the City University of New York, where he is Distinguished Professor Emeritus of Anthropology.

His *Genealogies of Religion* (1993) criticised the idea that "religion" is a universal category defined by private belief, and showed how it grew out of Christian and modern European history. His essay "The Idea of an Anthropology of Islam" (1986) proposed that Islam be studied as a discursive tradition, in which practices and arguments are linked to the past and to a community's debates about what is right. In *Formations of the Secular* (2003), *On Suicide Bombing* (2007) and later essays he examined secularism, the modern state, law, violence and human rights.
