Muhammad Husayn Fadlallah

محمد حسين فضل الله

1935–2010 · Lebanon · Shia marja, author and founder of the Mabarrat charitable association in Beirut

Muhammad Husayn Fadlallah was born in 1935 in Najaf, Iraq, into a family from southern Lebanon. After early schooling that included Qur’anic reading he entered the seminary of Najaf, where he studied for about twenty years and completed his formal training in the mid-1960s.

He later settled in Lebanon and became a leading Shia cleric in Beirut, where he held the rank of marja from 1989 until his death in 2010. He founded the Mabarrat association, which runs religious schools, a public library, a women’s cultural centre and a medical clinic. Media often described him as the spiritual mentor of Hezbollah, a label that he and other observers contested.

Fadlallah wrote many books in Arabic, from early works on Qur’anic method and Islamic concepts, through studies of the Islamic movement and Islamic civilisation, to books on women in Islamic law and society, commentary on the Qur’an and works on jurisprudence. He was widely known for independent rulings and for a rather open approach to contemporary questions.
