Süleyman Ateş was born in 1933 in the village of Tadım, south of Elazığ in eastern Turkey. He memorised the Qur'an as a child, studied Arabic with local teachers, completed the imam-hatip school in Elazığ in 1960 and then graduated from the Faculty of Theology at Ankara University with top marks. He became an assistant lecturer there in 1965, began doctoral work in 1968 and was appointed professor in 1978.

Between 1976 and 1978 he served as President of Religious Affairs (Diyanet İşleri Başkanı) of the Republic of Turkey, then returned to Ankara University. He did research in Germany at the Ruhr University Bochum and taught Qur'anic exegesis at the Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University in Riyadh and, for a year, at the Emir Abdelkader University in Constantine, Algeria. After heading departments of fundamental Islamic sciences at Ondokuz Mayıs University and Istanbul University he retired in 1999, and in 2001-02 taught and briefly directed the European Islamic University in the Netherlands.

Ateş is best known for his Turkish Qur'an translation and commentary, Yüce Kur'ân'ın Çağdaş Tefsiri, a work that reads the Qur'an in a contemporary idiom. His other books include a study of Sufi and allegorical (ishari) exegesis, a short manual of Islamic practice, works on the Qur'anic order and on religious truth, and a memoir. He has also written regularly for newspapers and answered readers' questions on a personal website.
