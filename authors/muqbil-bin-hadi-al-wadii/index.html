Muqbil bin Hadi al-Wadi'i

مقبل بن هادي الوادعي

1933–2001 · Yemen · Salafi hadith scholar; founder of Dar al-Hadith in Dammaj

Muqbil ibn Hadi al-Wadi’i was born around 1933 in the village of Dammaj, near Sa’da in northern Yemen; the exact date is not recorded. After learning reading, writing and Qur’an recitation in his village, he spent roughly two decades studying in Saudi Arabia, in Mecca and then at the Islamic University of Madinah, where he is said to have completed a master’s degree in hadith. His teachers included Abd al-Aziz ibn Baz and Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani.

Returning to Yemen, he established Dar al-Hadith al-Khayriyya in Dammaj, a Salafi teaching institute which drew students from many countries and became an important centre for Salafi learning in Yemen. He was detained in Saudi Arabia in 1979 on suspicion of connection with the seizure of the Grand Mosque in Mecca, was released and went back to Yemen. He died on 21 July 2001 in Jeddah after a long illness, and was buried in Mecca, after a funeral prayer at the Sacred Mosque. The institute in Dammaj was closed in 2014 after a siege by the Houthi movement.

His writings are in Arabic. They include works of hadith authentication and criticism, notably his collections of authentic hadith not found in the two Sahihs, a study of the authentic reports on the occasions of Qur’anic revelation, as well as doctrinal tracts and polemics against Shi’ism, communism, and rival Islamic movements, and collections of fatwas and answers to questions.
