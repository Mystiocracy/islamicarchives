Asma Lamrabet was born in Rabat in 1961 and trained as a medical doctor. From 1995 she worked for about eight years as a volunteer physician in Spain and Latin America, mainly in Chile and Mexico, where she came into contact with liberation theology, an encounter that led her to examine her own religious tradition more closely.

After returning to Morocco in the mid-2000s she brought together a group of Muslim women interested in studying Islam and intercultural dialogue. In 2008 she became president of the International Group of Study and Reflection on Women in Islam (GIERFI), based in Barcelona, and in 2011 she was named director of the Centre for Studies and Research on Women's Issues in Islam, attached to Morocco's Rabita Mohammadia of Ulema.

Her books, written mainly in French and later translated into English and Spanish, offer a rereading of the Qur'an, the biography of the Prophet's wives and the question of women's status in Islam, arguing that patriarchal later interpretation, rather than the foundational texts, explains inequality. She is often placed in a Moroccan "third way" of Islamic feminism that works within the religious sources and distances itself from both Western feminism and conservative readings. Critics have argued that her approach does not engage enough with questions such as violence against women and polygamy, or with the technical methods of the classical sciences. Her book on women and men in the Qur'an received an Arab Woman Organization award in 2013.
