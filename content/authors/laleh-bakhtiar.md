Laleh Bakhtiar was an Iranian-American writer and psychologist born in Tehran in 1938. She took a history degree at Chatham College in Pittsburgh and a doctorate in educational psychology at the University of New Mexico, and she studied Sufism and Qur'anic Arabic in Iran under Seyyed Hossein Nasr. She later lived in Chicago, where she led the Institute of Traditional Psychoethics and Guidance and worked with Kazi Publications.

Her own books deal with Sufism, Islamic spirituality, moral psychology and law. They include *Sufi: Expressions of the Mystic Quest* (1976), *The Sense of Unity* (1973, with the architect Nader Ardalan), a series of handbooks on traditional psychoethics, *Sufi Women of America* (1996) and a one-volume compendium of the views of the major legal schools. She also wrote on the Companions of the Prophet and on Ramadan.

She was best known publicly for *The Sublime Quran* (2007), an English translation that rendered a disputed verse on wife-beating differently from earlier translators and attracted both praise and criticism. She also produced translations of classical authors such as al-Ghazali and Ibn Sina. She died in Chicago in 2020.
