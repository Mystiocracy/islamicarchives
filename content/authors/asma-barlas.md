Asma Barlas was born in Lahore in 1950 and studied at Kinnaird College and the University of the Punjab before taking a master's degree and doctorate at the University of Denver. In 1976 she was among the first women admitted to Pakistan's foreign service, and she received political asylum in the United States in 1983.

She joined the politics department at Ithaca College in 1991 and directed its Center for the Study of Culture, Race, and Ethnicity for twelve years. In 2008 she held the Spinoza Chair in Philosophy at the University of Amsterdam.

Her first book dealt with colonial legacies and militarism in South Asian politics. She is best known for work on Qur'anic interpretation, arguing that the Qur'an itself does not support patriarchal readings and that such readings reflect the habits of interpreters. She prefers not to be described as an Islamic feminist unless that term means a discourse of equality and justice drawn from the Qur'an.
