Emad Hamdeh is an American academic working on Islamic law, intellectual history and modern Muslim reform movements. He has taught as an assistant professor of Arabic and Islamic studies at Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University.

His research examines debates over religious authority in modern Islam, particularly the tensions between Salafi reformers and adherents of the traditional legal schools. He has published journal articles on these themes, including a study of the hadith scholar Nasir al-Din al-Albani and his critics.

His books include *The Necessity of Hadith in Islam* (International Islamic Publishing House, 2011) and *Salafism and Traditionalism: Scholarly Authority in Modern Islam* (Cambridge University Press, 2021). He has contributed research to Yaqeen Institute.
