Tariq Ramadan was born in Geneva in 1962. His father, Saʿid Ramadan, was a leader of the Muslim Brotherhood in exile, and his maternal grandfather was Hasan al-Banna, the movement's founder. He studied philosophy and French literature at the University of Geneva and wrote his doctorate there on Hasan al-Banna, and he also studied classical Islamic sciences, including at al-Azhar in Cairo.

He became widely known for arguing that Muslims in Europe and North America should see themselves as full citizens, not as a minority apart. In books such as *To Be a European Muslim* (1999) and *Western Muslims and the Future of Islam* (2004) he argued for a "Western Muslim" identity rooted in the sources of Islam and in the societies where Muslims live. His *Radical Reform: Islamic Ethics and Liberation* (2009) called for reform that begins with the interpretation of ethics. He held academic posts at Fribourg and at Oxford, where he was Professor of Contemporary Islamic Studies, and he lectured widely in Europe and the Muslim world. His public positions were often debated, and he was refused entry to the United States for several years until 2010.

In 2018 he was accused of sexual assault and took leave from Oxford. He has faced legal proceedings in France and Switzerland, and in 2024 a Geneva court convicted him in his absence. He has denied the allegations. This archive lists his writings as intellectual works and does not assess those proceedings.
