Ovamir Anjum is a scholar of Islamic intellectual history. Born in Karachi, Pakistan, he grew up between Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and the United States, receiving early religious instruction at home and later studying fiqh, legal theory and Qur'anic recitation with teachers from South Asian, Azhari and Syrian backgrounds. His university training began in nuclear engineering and physics and in computer science at the University of Wisconsin-Madison; he then took a master's in social sciences at the University of Chicago and a PhD in history at Wisconsin-Madison.

He holds the Imam Khattab Endowed Chair of Islamic Studies in the Department of Philosophy and Religious Studies at the University of Toledo. His research sits where theology, ethics, law and politics meet, and draws comparisons with Western political thought. He has also founded the Ummatics Institute.

His books include *Politics, Law and Community in Islamic Thought: The Taymiyyan Moment* (Cambridge University Press, 2012) and a translation of Ibn al-Qayyim's *Madārij al-sālikīn*, published as *Ranks of the Divine Seekers* (Brill, 2020). At Yaqeen Institute he serves as editor-in-chief and has contributed essays, commentary and podcast appearances.
