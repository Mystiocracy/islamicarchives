History of

Morocco

6 periods · 661 – today. Each period below is a power that ruled all or part of what is now Morocco.

  1. 661–750

    Umayyad Caliphate

    Damascus

    The first hereditary dynasty. Its rule stretched from Spain and North Africa to Sindh and Central Asia. Arabic became the language of administration and the Dome of the Rock was built.

    Rulers: Muʿāwiya I (661–680) · ʿAbd al-Malik (685–705) · al-Walīd I (705–715) · ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz (717–720) · Hishām (724–743) · Marwān II (744–750)

    Further reading ↗

  2. 909–1171

    Fatimid Caliphate

    Mahdia, then Cairo

    An Ismāʿīlī Shiʿi caliphate that claimed descent from Fāṭima. It ruled North Africa, Egypt and parts of Syria and Arabia. Cairo was founded in 969 and al-Azhar around 970.

    Rulers: ʿUbayd Allāh al-Mahdī (909–934) · al-Muʿizz (953–975) · al-ʿAzīz (975–996) · al-Ḥākim (996–1021) · al-Mustanṣir (1036–1094) · al-ʿĀḍid (1160–1171)

    Further reading ↗

  3. 1040–1269

    Almoravids and Almohads

    Marrakesh, Seville

    Two Berber reform movements ruled Morocco and Muslim Spain in succession. The Almoravids held power from 1040 to 1147 and the Almohads from 1121 to 1269. Ibn Rushd and Ibn Ṭufayl lived under the Almohads.

    Rulers: Yūsuf ibn Tāshfīn (1061–1106) · ʿAbd al-Muʾmin (1130–1163) · Yaʿqūb al-Manṣūr (1184–1199)

    Further reading ↗

  4. 1830–1913

    European colonial rule and the Nahḍa

    Algiers, Cairo, Delhi

    France took Algeria in 1830, Britain took over India in 1858 and occupied Egypt in 1882. Reformers such as al-Afghānī and ʿAbduh responded with the Nahḍa and new Islamic thought.

    Rulers: Muḥammad ʿAlī Pasha (1805–1848) · Emir ʿAbd al-Qādir (1832–1847) · Abdulhamid II (1876–1909)

  5. 1947–1978

    New states and revival

    Karachi, Cairo, Jakarta

    Pakistan and Indonesia and most Arab and African states won independence. Islamic revival movements such as the Muslim Brotherhood and Jamāʿat-i Islāmī grew. The Organisation of Islamic Cooperation was founded in 1969.

    Rulers: Muhammad Ali Jinnah (1947–1948) · Sukarno (1945–1967) · Gamal Abdel Nasser (1956–1970) · Faisal ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz (1964–1975)

  6. 2000–today

    Contemporary voices

    Worldwide

    Writing in the age of globalisation and the internet. The Arab uprisings began in 2010–11, and Muslim intellectuals now write for global audiences in many languages.
