Fethullah Gülen was a Turkish Muslim preacher and writer born near Erzurum in eastern Anatolia; official documents give 1941 as his birth year, though an earlier date is sometimes cited. He studied in religious schools in Erzurum and was influenced by the writings of Said Nursi. After becoming a state-licensed preacher he served as an imam in Edirne and later in Izmir, and retired from official preaching in 1981.

He became the inspiration of a movement, known as Gülen or Hizmet, that built schools, media outlets, charities and dialogue organisations in Turkey and abroad, and he helped found the Journalists and Writers Foundation in 1994. He moved to the United States in the late 1990s. After the attempted coup in Turkey in 2016 the Turkish government accused him and his followers of responsibility; he denied any involvement, and the movement has been designated a terrorist organisation by Turkey.

His writings, which are mostly collections of articles, sermons and essays originally in Turkish and widely translated, discuss faith and the Qur'an, Sufi ethics, the life of the Prophet, education, tolerance and interfaith dialogue. The English translations listed here are by various translators. He died in Pennsylvania in 2024.
