Wahiduddin Khan, usually called Maulana Wahiduddin Khan, was born in 1925 in the village of Badharia in the Azamgarh district of what is now Uttar Pradesh, India. His father died when he was a small child and he was raised by his mother and an uncle. He studied the traditional dars-i nizami curriculum at the Madrasatul Islah in Sarai Mir, graduating in 1944.

In his early career he worked within the Jamaat-e-Islami, rising to its central consultative council, but in the early 1960s he left the movement. He felt that its political reading of Islam was unsuited to Muslims living as a minority in India, and he set out his objections in a critique of the party's founding thought. From then on he argued that the core of religion is faith, devotion and invitation to God (dawah), and that Muslims should avoid political confrontation, concentrating instead on education, work and peaceful coexistence.

He founded the Islamic Centre in New Delhi in 1970 and started the monthly Al-Risala in Urdu in 1976, followed by English and Hindi editions. In 2001 he set up the Centre for Peace and Spirituality, which also publishes through Goodword Books. His output runs to well over a hundred titles in Urdu and English on faith and reason, the Prophet's life, non-violence, jihad, spiritual development and Islam's place in the modern world, together with an Urdu Qur'an commentary, Tazkirul Quran, and an English translation and commentary.

He received the Padma Bhushan in 2000 and the Padma Vibhushan in 2021, and was widely known internationally as an advocate of interfaith dialogue. His views on politics, jihad and Indian Muslim affairs were sometimes strongly contested by other Muslim scholars and movements. He died in Delhi in April 2021.
