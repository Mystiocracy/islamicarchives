Subjects

Education

Learning, institutions and the reform of education.

From al-Tahtawi's mission to Paris in the 1820s to the madrasa reforms of the late nineteenth century, Muslim writers have returned to schools: what to teach, in what language, and who should run them. Sayyid Ahmad Khan's college at Aligarh and Shibli Nomani's Nadwat al-ʿUlamaʾ gave different answers in India. Muhammad Qutb wrote on Islamic upbringing, and Bakr Abu Zayd on the manners of the student of knowledge.

Many of the entries are short talks and pamphlets by Abul Hasan Ali Nadwi on school curricula and on teachers.
